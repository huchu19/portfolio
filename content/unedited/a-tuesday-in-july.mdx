<Cut>Rain again. Rain still. I have written the word rain in this file four times and deleted it three.</Cut>

Rain since morning, the indecisive London kind that can't commit to being weather. Two rejection emails before lunch, both "impressed by my background," both moving forward with other candidates. I've started reading them the way you read horoscopes — vaguely, for tone.

<Doubt>The horoscope line is the only joke in the paragraph and I'm not sure it earns being the only joke. It was "the way you read terms and conditions" for about an hour, which was worse.</Doubt>

<Cut>The second one came from a company whose careers page says they're "building the future of work." I have been building the future of my own work for eleven months and the future of work appears to be an automated email at 11:40 on a Tuesday.</Cut>

But: I wrote one good function today. Genuinely good. Small, obvious in hindsight, the kind you delete twenty lines to earn. Nobody will ever notice it, which I've decided is the definition of craft.

<Doubt>"Which I've decided is the definition of craft" is the sentence I'd cut first if someone made me lose a line. It's tidy. Tidy is usually where I'm hiding.</Doubt>

The negotiation continues between the two people I currently am — the one formatting CVs and the one who built something at midnight just to see if it would work. They share a laptop. They are learning to share a calendar. The midnight one is better company, but the CV one pays for the coffee.

<Cut>Neither of them is doing the washing up.</Cut>

Made biryani from the recipe in my head, which is really my mother's recipe with errors. Called home. Didn't mention the emails. Some ledgers you keep only for yourself.

<Doubt>I nearly cut the whole last paragraph. Not because it's bad — because it's true, and my mother reads this site.</Doubt>

Tomorrow: the portfolio site. The irony of building a page called *Now* while being between things is not lost on me. Maybe that's what *now* always is.
