There is a specific kind of arithmetic you only do at departure gates. Not the time-zone kind — the other kind. What did I leave at my grandmother's house that I meant to bring? How many months until the next time? Why does the air conditioning at Allama Iqbal International always smell faintly of cardamom and jet fuel, and why do I miss it before I've even left?

## LHE → DXB

The first leg is always the loud one. Families flying together, plastic bags of food that the cabin crew pretend not to see, a man two rows ahead arguing gently about a window seat he was promised. I love this flight. It is impossible to feel like a stranger on it.

## The layover

Dubai at 3 a.m. is a shopping mall wearing an airport as a disguise. I found a quiet gate, opened my laptop, and did not write a single line of code. Instead I wrote most of what became [On Leaving Comfort](/writing/on-leaving-comfort) — apparently the departure lounge is where my poetry lives now.

---

## DXB → LHR

Somewhere over Turkey the cabin went dark and I watched the flight map instead of a film, the little plane inching across places I've never stood. Eleven hours is long enough to hold both feelings at once: the pull backwards and the pull forwards. I don't think they cancel out. I think you just learn to fly with both.

Heathrow, passport control, the specific grey of a London morning. Home again. Also: away again. Both true.
