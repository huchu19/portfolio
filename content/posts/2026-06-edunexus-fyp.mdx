EduNexus is my final-year project at Queen Mary — an answer to the fragmentation problem in digital education. Teachers today live across a CMS, a quiz tool, and a grade spreadsheet with no data flowing between them; students get one-size-fits-all content and no picture of what they actually understand.

## What it does

A teacher types a prompt; Gemini 2.5 Flash generates lesson slides, flashcards, and quiz questions the teacher can review and edit before anything reaches a student. Quizzes adapt difficulty per student, and the metacognitive question types — explain your reasoning, analyse this error, transfer this idea — capture *how* someone thinks, not just whether they clicked the right radio button.

On the other side, the AI extracts concepts and prerequisites from lessons into a force-directed knowledge graph that shows per-student mastery and flags blind spots. XP, streaks, badges, and an open learner model give students a dashboard of their own understanding — score trends, topic mastery, confidence calibration.

## The part I care about

The feature that reframed the project is **Teach Mode**: the student explains a concept to an AI playing a confused learner, and the system listens instead of lecturing. I wrote about what that did to my thinking in [the EduNexus essay](/writing/what-i-learned-building-edunexus).

## Under the hood

Next.js App Router frontend, FastAPI + SQLAlchemy backend on PostgreSQL, JWT auth, pytest and Playwright for tests. Gemini 2.5 Flash is the primary model with GPT-4o-mini as fallback — the abstraction that made model-swapping boring was worth every hour it cost.
